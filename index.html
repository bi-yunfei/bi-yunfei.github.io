Delay-Tolerant Formation Tracking for Multi-AGV Systems via Reference-Shift Coupling

Yunfei Bi

arXiv preprint, 2026 · research internship at Siemens Technology Center Garching, 2025

AGVs that carry a load together have to keep their formation when one of them slips or stops, while they talk over Wi-Fi with delays that differ per link, vary over time and jump. In the cascade design, delayed neighbour data only drive a filter that shifts each vehicle's own reference; the tracking law never sees the network, so stability holds for every bounded delay.

  • Theory: tracking and formation errors converge for any bounded delay and any directed, switching graph; a strict Lyapunov function with explicit constants, exponential convergence and an input-to-state estimate with delay-independent gains, and a formula for how much the coupling reduces the formation error. The proofs were reviewed independently and checked numerically.
  • High-fidelity simulation: 4,894 runs with rigid-body vehicle dynamics, PI wheel loops with limits, slip, LiDAR–odometry localisation, clock offsets and Wi-Fi burst loss, including paired comparisons against three tuned baselines; every run is deterministic and reproduces bit for bit.
  • Results: the cascade never failed (latency up to 4 s, 90 % packet loss, 12 vehicles); it cut the formation error after a safety stop by 47 % and after traction losses by 21–24 %, at the price of tracking accuracy.

CommonRoad-Game: A Human-in-the-Loop Simulation Framework for Autonomous Driving

Yunfei Bi, Youran Wang

arXiv preprint, 2026 · semester thesis at TUM

Motion planners should be tested with humans in the loop, yet existing simulators often replay recorded datasets, lack interfaces for real-time human interaction, are only loosely tied to an autonomous-driving ecosystem, or are too heavy for rapid prototyping. CommonRoad-Game is a lightweight human-in-the-loop simulation framework tightly integrated with CommonRoad, for testing motion planners with human participants and for analyzing human driving in interactive scenarios.

  • Time synchronization: a multi-threaded architecture aligns simulation time with wall-clock time, so autonomous and human-driven vehicles interact deterministically and consistently in time; the drift drops from 402 ms to 1.5 ms.
  • Scenario generation: driving logs recorded during human-in-the-loop experiments become diverse, reproducible test cases.
  • Results: stable temporal synchronization, scalable multi-agent simulation, and seamless integration of CommonRoad-compatible motion planners such as IDM and a sampling-based reactive planner.
